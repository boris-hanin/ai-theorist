Mixture-of-Experts in the Neural Mean ODE limit

Hyperparameter transfer across five scaling dials, and what the fixed-FLOP shape landscape actually looks like once you write the constraint down correctly.

Merges 2601.20205 (MoE) with 2509.10167 / 2603.18168 (Mean ODE) Derivation preregistered before measurement

The claim

Only active experts fire, so the effective width is L a M

Units are indexed by the triple (i, j, l) over experts × expert-units × layers. But an inactive expert contributes exactly zero to a token’s forward pass, so it cannot enter that token’s central limit theorem. The averaging therefore runs over a · M · L units — the number of active experts, not the number of experts.

W_eff = L · a · M   →   error = O( 1/L  +  √(D / L a M)  +  1/√D )

The expert count E is absent from the rate entirely. It changes the limit object — capacity — not the rate of approach to it. Every panel below is a consequence of that one sentence.

Transfer is asymptotic, so the column that matters is the drift over the largest three values — and every dial lands at or below 0.07 decades there. E at fixed a is the dial the theory says is invisible to the rate, and it is flat over the full range.

A single global learning rate is wrong in D, and this test is what exposed it. The three parameter groups have different D-counting. Measured with one global lr = L M a D η, the induced change in each group’s own observable scales as D−0.18 (down-projection), D+1.28 (up-projection) and D+1.37 (router) instead of D0 — the router and up-projection updates blow up with embedding dimension. An earlier version of this page reported D transfer as passing at 0.22 decades; that was wrong, and the D curve was in fact accelerating away (increments +0.08, −0.05, −0.17), not converging.
lr_U = L M a / D   ·   lr_W = L M a D   ·   lr_R = L a √M / D

Derived from the coherent (LLN-alignment) labelling that governs the trained regime. The spread between the up- and down-projection rates is D². With these, the D curve changes character: increments become +0.14, +0.06, +0.03, +0.01 — converging, not diverging. The one-step shift is exactly −2.0 for both mis-scaled groups, as the derivation intends.

Experiment 2

Depth, active experts and expert width are interchangeable

If the effective width really is the product L a M, then holding that product fixed and redistributing it among the three should change nothing. Ten splits at L a M = 256, spanning a = 2…16 and L = 2…64:

MLU init — spread 1.10× fan-in control — spread 3.90×

This was the preregistered risky step. The a active experts all share the routing-conditioning event, which correlates them; had that correlation been order one, the averaging would have been coherent and the effective width would be L M, not L a M. It is not, and the law stands.

The (L=8, a=2, M=16) split has M = D = 16, where the two initializations are literally the same model. The two arms agree there to every printed digit — an identity control landing inside the sweep.

Experiment 3

The fixed-FLOP shape landscape — and a correction

MoE has two budgets: parameters scale as L E M D but FLOPs only as L a M D, because inactive experts are stored and not computed. The rate tracks the FLOP budget C.

The measurement corrected the derivation. An earlier version balanced all three error terms and reported L = C^(1/6) as the optimum. But substituting the constraint, the CLT term becomes D/√C — independent of L entirely, because fixing C and D pins the product L aM. The flat measured line above is exactly that. So L appears in only one of the three terms and can only help.
D = Θ(C^1/3)   binding  ·  L ≥ Θ(C^1/6)   a floor, not an optimum  ·  error = O(C^-1/6)

The honest statement is that D = Θ(C1/3) is the genuinely binding requirement; L = C^(1/6) is the shallowest depth achieving the optimal rate, not a unique optimum, and deeper is not worse until aM hits its floor. At that shallowest depth aM = C1/2, and how that splits between expert count and expert width is free.

Which is why MoE works, in one line: at fixed FLOPs you may raise E at fixed a — buying capacity in the limit object at the cost of parameters, but of neither FLOPs nor convergence rate.

Scope

What is not established

An unexplained residual. The per-group learning rates are derived from the coherent labelling, so at initialization the one-step exponents should sit exactly 1/2 lower for the up-projection and router (the F18 signature). Measured: −0.72 and −0.64 against a predicted −0.50, and −0.18 for the down-projection against 0. A common ~0.2 offset across all three groups that I have not accounted for. The asymptotic transfer above is unaffected, but the one-step counting is not fully closed.

The DMFT for this limit is derived — three response pairs per expert-layer, including a router pair with no dense counterpart — but there is no solver, so the memoryless-limit claim is untested. Parameterization and limit structure are validated; the dynamics are not. The C^(1/6) rate also inherits an earlier caveat: the 1/√D term it depends on was only suggestively measured, because the regression problem itself changes with D.
