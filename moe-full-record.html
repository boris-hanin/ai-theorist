Section 1

Architecture

Five independent scale dimensions: depth L, embedding D, expert count E, active experts per token a, and expert hidden width M. Sparsity κ = a/E is held fixed, following 2601.20205 §3.2 — scaling E at fixed a takes κ → 0 and is a different problem.

h&sup0; = W_embed x x ∈ R^d₀, d₀ = 8 hˡ⁺¹ = hˡ + c_L · (1/a) · ∑_{i ∈ A(hˡ)} g_i(hˡ) · E_i(hˡ) E_i(h) = ∑_{j=1}^{M} w^{ij} · tanh( ⟨u^{ij}, h⟩ ) a 2-layer perceptron, M units g_i(h) = σ( ⟨r_i, h⟩ ) mixing coefficient A(h) = top_a( { g_i(h) + b_i } ) hard routing, no-grad f(x) = ⟨w_out, h^L⟩ scalar readout

The expert biases b_i enter only the hard top-a and receive no gradient. They are updated by the auxiliary-loss-free load-balancing rule of 2601.20205 Eq. (2):

b_i ← b_i − η_bias · ( Load_i − κ ) η_bias = Θ(1)

Measured over 25 steps, this genuinely balances and has a finite stable range: max imbalance max_i|Load_i−κ| goes 0.750→0.750 at η_bias=0 (control), 0.750→0.250 at 0.3 and 1.0, and back to 0.750 at 3.0 (overshoot).

Fidelity — what this is, and what it is not

This is the reduced MoE-only model of 2601.20205 §4, not the §3.1 transformer, and not Chizat’s model unmodified. Every deviation, stated in full:
  • No multi-head self-attention and no LayerNorm.
  • Plain gradient descent, not Adam or its SignGD proxy.
  • Linear readout ⟨w,h^L⟩ rather than W_unembd φ(h^L).
  • From Chizat: an embedding and scalar readout were added (his model is R^D → R^D), because the rate test needs a D-comparable observable; and per-group learning rates replace his single global one — forced by convention, since he bounds ‖h‖=Θ(1) while this program uses per-coordinate Θ(1), under which one global LR is provably wrong in D.
What is validated is the scaling structure of the merged limit on this reduced architecture — not a transformer with attention, LayerNorm and Adam.

The two entries that are not standard

The down-projection. With c_L = 1/(LM) and σ_w = 1, the residual scale — branch multiplier times output-layer norm — is √D/(LM), which is Chizat’s necessary-and-sufficient condition for maximal local feature updates. Written instead with a 1/L branch, it is σ_down = √D/M, which is exactly 2601.20205’s Table 1 entry α_ffn⁻¹ n⁻½. The two papers’ conditions coincide, by two independent routes. Against ordinary fan-in M^−½ the ratio is

σ_down(MLU) / σ_down(fan-in) = √(D/M) → identical iff M = Θ(D)

so CompleteP is the M = Θ(D) slice and says nothing off it. Real FFNs run M = 4D; MoE expert-width multipliers push far further.

What the init actually buys — and what it does not. It does not change the update: under this parameterization the block’s absolute update is M·(base/M)·Θ(1), free of σ_down. It changes the initial contribution: σ_down√M is 1 under fan-in (M-independent) but √(D/M) under MLU (vanishing in M). Accumulated over L blocks the init deviation is 1/√L versus √(D/(LaM)). So the MLU init is precisely what makes block width enter the mean-field average at all. Under fan-in the effective width is L; under MLU it is L·a·M. Measured: at fixed LaM, MLU spread across ten splits is 1.10×, fan-in 3.90×.

Section 3

Heuristic derivation

3.1  What the mean-field average runs over

Units are indexed by the triple (i,j,l) ∈ [E]×[M]×[L]. But an inactive expert contributes exactly zero to a token’s forward pass, so it cannot enter that token’s central limit theorem. Per layer a·M units fire, so

effective width W_eff = L · a · M — with a, NOT E

The expert count E does not appear in the rate at all. It changes the limit object — capacity — not the rate of approach to it.

3.2  Splitting the block into drift and fluctuation

c_L (1/a) ∑_{i∈A} g_i E_i = c_L · M · Ē(h,s) ← coherent: the drift + c_L (1/a) ∑_{i∈A, j} [ g_i w^{ij} φ_ij − mean ] ← a·M-term CLT where Ē(h,s) = E[ g w φ | active ] is a CONDITIONAL expectation, the conditioning being the routing event q ≥ q*(κ).
  • Drift. The M units inside one expert share a mean, so they add coherently: c_L·M·Ē. A nontrivial L→∞ ODE needs this Θ(1/L), giving c_L = 1/(LM).
  • Fluctuation. (1/a)√(aM)·s_w√D, times c_L, accumulated incoherently over L layers, gives √(D/(LaM)).

3.3  The rate

‖ ResNet-MoE − limit ‖ = O( 1/L + √(D/(L a M)) + 1/√D ) ↑ ↑ ↑ Euler a·M·L site-CLT D-coordinate mean-field

Only two combinations enter, L and LaM. Consequences: a and M are exchangeable (the MoE analogue of Chizat’s depth/width exchange); E is absent; and depth is special, appearing in both terms, so it buys discretization and averaging while a and M buy only averaging.

3.4  Two budgets, and the optimal shape

MoE stores all experts but computes only the active ones:

parameters P = Θ(L E M D) FLOPs C = Θ(L a M D)

The rate tracks the FLOP budget. Substituting aM = C/(LD), the CLT term becomes D/√C — independent of L, because fixing C and D pins the product L·aM. So the three terms are

T₁ = a/L T₂ = b·D/√C T₃ = c/√D
A correction the experiment forced. An earlier version balanced all three terms and reported L = C^(1/6) as the optimum. That is wrong: T₂ does not depend on L at all. Measured directly — at C = 8192, D = 16, sweeping L = 2→64 with aM = 256→8 — the CLT term is flat (1.62e-2 → 1.49e-2). Balancing T₂ against T₃ over D and then requiring T₁ not to dominate gives the honest statement:
D = Θ(C^1/3) binding L ≥ Θ(C^1/6) a FLOOR, not an optimum — deeper is not worse a M = C/(L D) the split between expert count and width is FREE error = O(C^−1/6)

Which is why MoE works, in one line: at fixed FLOPs you may raise E at fixed a — buying capacity in the limit object at the cost of parameters, but of neither FLOPs nor convergence rate.

Section 4

Transfer experiments

4.1  How a transfer verdict is judged

Three bars, because any one alone gives the wrong answer:

  • Statistical — the optimum is located per seed; drift within seed scatter is not evidence.
  • Practical — 0.3 decades (a factor 2 in learning rate). Five seeds resolve a 0.04-decade drift, which is real but is not what anyone means by a transfer failure.
  • Shape — max − min cannot tell a drift that is settling from one running away, and transfer is an asymptotic claim. A sub-threshold drift whose spread over the largest three dial values exceeds its spread over the smallest three returns SUSPECT, never a pass.
The shape bar exists because it was learned the hard way. The embedding-D dial once read +1.05 +1.13 +1.08 +0.91 — drift 0.22, under the bar, reported as a pass — while its increments were +0.08, −0.05, −0.17, accelerating away. The true cause was a genuinely wrong parameterization (one global LR for three groups). In the same table the L and a dials were justified as finite-size because they flatten, and that verdict was then applied to a curve doing the opposite.

The fifth dial is the paper’s own caveat, not a new failure. It holds a fixed while raising E, i.e. takes κ = a/E → 0, which 2601.20205 §3.2 explicitly excludes: they scale n_exp and n_act together preserving κ, and warn that HPs will not transfer across different κ. The drift appears precisely when the κ-dependent balancing rule is active and precisely in the dial that varies κ. Every dial inside their scaling regime transfers.

4.3  Depth, active experts and expert width are interchangeable

If the effective width really is the product LaM, holding it fixed and redistributing it among the three must change nothing. Ten splits at LaM = 256 spanning a = 2…16 and L = 2…64, measured at initialization (no optimizer confound): MLU spread 1.10×, fan-in control 3.90×.

This was the preregistered risky step. The a active experts all share the routing-conditioning event, which correlates them; had that correlation been order one, the averaging would be coherent and the effective width would be LM. It is not. The (L=8, a=2, M=16) split has M = D = 16, where the two initializations are literally the same model — an identity control landing inside the sweep, agreeing to every printed digit.

The 1/√D term took four attempts, three of which were contaminated. All the failures measured it as a bias — a gap to a reference. Section 6

What is open

6.1  1/√D degrades with training time

The law is exact at initialization and LaM-independent across an 8× change in aM, out to D = 16384. It is not exact after long training.

The DMFT mechanism. In the single-site system the kernel C_h = (1/D)⟨h(t),h(s)⟩ is a population average over the D coordinate-sites, so 1/√D is the site-CLT. Under training the sites are not independent: they couple through two shared scalars, the error trajectory Δ(t) = y − f(t) and the readout w. And Δ is itself a population average, so it fluctuates at 1/√D while entering every site coherently:

δK = [ site-CLT ] + (∂K/∂Δ) · δΔ ~ 1/√D ~ (∂K/∂Δ)/√D ← shared, rank-1, does NOT average over sites

Both are 1/√D at fixed susceptibility. ∂K/∂Δ is the Δ-loop gain, and it accumulates with training.

My specific hypothesis was falsified. I predicted the cause was mismatched training progress — at fixed step count larger-D models train further (loss 0.3930→0.3741 across D; loss-matching needs 22→19 steps). Matching by loss instead of step count tests it, and it only holds shallow: −0.4980 at loss 0.40 (~20 steps), but −0.4454 at 0.30 (~40 steps) and −0.3743 at 0.20 (~52 steps) — tracking the fixed-step values at the same step counts. So the controlling variable is elapsed training, not training progress. Why the susceptibility is D-dependent is not established.

Consequence for the rate: it stands. The rate test runs at 8 and 24 steps, where the deviation is ≤ 0.001 and ~0.01–0.04. The failure lives at horizons longer than the rate test uses — the caveat is real, but bounded, and does not reach the headline.

6.2  Not attempted

  • No DMFT solver. The single-site system of derivation 09 §5–§7 — three response pairs per expert-layer, including a router pair with no dense counterpart — is derived but unsolved, so the memoryless-limit claim (response sector O(1/LaM)) is untested. Parameterization and limit structure are validated; the dynamics are not.
  • The lazy branch α* = (ML)1/4 remains inconclusive: comparing a ResNet to its own linearization cancels the CLT term by shared initialization, and a large linearized reference carries its own uncontrolled α-dependent error.
  • Attention, LayerNorm, Adam and a nonlinear unembedding — the remaining distance to the §3.1 transformer.
